# Heading torque (dθ/dt) & stability criterion — theory

Deep detail for the half-angle heading torque, the `K=2` default, the two stability criteria, and the coupled-Jacobian correctness proofs in `decision_model.py`. Auto-loads with `decision_model.py`. CLAUDE.md keeps the one-line summaries; the derivations live here.

## Self-consistent equilibria — the three approaches considered

At a physical turning equilibrium the observer has stopped turning, so heading = allocentric consensus direction ⇒ egocentric consensus = 0 ⇒ (for any reasonable warp) Θ_neural = 0 ⇒ γ = R + 0j. Three approaches to handling neural warping under self-consistency were considered; **Option 3 was chosen:**

1. ~~Make warping allocentric.~~ Rejected: experimental evidence shows the denser neural band near the center of the visual field is intrinsically egocentric. The warping has to be egocentric.
2. ~~Hybrid with mismatch from Hamiltonian.~~ Rejected: suboptimal, no good error estimates.
3. **Accept heading-dependent equilibria and find the self-consistent ones.** Adopted because we already accept that consensus direction should depend on where the observer is looking; the self-consistent solution is the physically meaningful subset.

The allocentric consensus direction *is* `θ` (the heading) at a self-consistent equilibrium — no inverse mapping needed to recover the physical direction.

## The two stability criteria (full detail)

Both are built from the same numerically-differenced 3×3 Jacobian on `(γ_re, γ_im, θ)` (`h=1e-6`, `tol=1e-8`), assembled once by [`_coupled_jacobian`](../../decision_model.py#L2759) and partitioned `J = [[A, b], [c, d]]` (A = the 2×2 γ block, b = ∂_θ(dγ/dt), c = ∂_γ(dθ/dt), d = ∂_θ(dθ/dt)).

- **`'reduced'` (DEFAULT, since 2026-06-08).** The timescale-separated ("block") test, consistent with γ slaved to equilibrium everywhere else in the model (mean field; γ run to steady state before each θ step in `plot_walkers`; no γ-noise). Stable iff **(1)** the fast γ block `A` is Hurwitz (the γ-equilibrium branch `γ=h(θ)` is attracting — this is exactly the `discrim_a` criterion, which since 2026-07 is the *full* fast-block test: transverse `A<1` **and** `det>0`) **AND (2)** the slow Schur complement `λ_slow = d − c·A⁻¹·b < 0` (the slaved heading flow `dθ/dt = g(h(θ))` is stable). `λ_slow` is the leading (λ→0) term of the exact eigenvalue equation `λ = d − c·(A − λI)⁻¹·b`; setting λ=0 inside the resolvent **is** the adiabatic elimination of γ. **The slow test is evaluated as `sign(det J)`, not by inverting A:** since `det(J) = det(A)·λ_slow` (block-determinant identity) and `det(A) > 0` for a Hurwitz 2×2 `A`, `λ_slow < 0 ⟺ det(J) < 0`. This stays well-conditioned at a **γ-fold** (`eig(A) → 0`), where `A⁻¹` — and hence `λ_slow` itself — diverges (`|λ_slow| → ∞`) even though the sign is unambiguous and `det(J)` stays bounded; the A-Hurwitz gate already rejects the genuinely singular case. Verdict-identical to the Schur form (0/538 mismatch on the island grid; the block-determinant identity is unit-tested). Implemented in [`NBM._discrim_reduced`](../../decision_model.py#L2805) / `IEM._discrim_reduced`. For NBM `d=0` exactly (dθ/dt has no explicit θ); for IEM `d=−K·R/2` at an SC eq (its torque depends on θ directly). The block-determinant-identity derivation is in [theory/block_determinant_identity.tex](../../theory/block_determinant_identity.tex).
- **`'discrim_a'`.** The **fast γ-block test alone** (`_discrim_A` / `_discrim_A_nu`): the free-energy Hessian is positive-definite ⇔ transverse `A<1` **and** `det>0` (`A`-Hurwitz), no slow-mode test. Kept for comparison plots. **Since 2026-07** it is the complete fast block; previously it tested only the transverse `A<1` and so *also* over-counted on the fast layer (radial folds + off-diagonal saddles). `_discrim_A` now adds the analytic `det>0`; `_discrim_A_nu` takes the block numerically from `_coupled_jacobian`. Derivation + the necessary-not-sufficient proof: [free_energy_derivation.md](../../theory/free_energy_derivation.md) §6.1.

`sc_equilib`, `gamma_equilib`, `_count_stable_at`, `_process_point`, `plot_direction_mesh`, and `plot_bifurcation_diagram` all accept both. (NBM only — `IEM.sc_equilib`/`gamma_equilib` return just gammas; the IEM plot/count helpers do their own stability test.) NBM and IEM bifurcation/mesh defaults are now `'reduced'` (IEM's was previously `'discrim_a'`).

**There is no fully-coupled criterion (`'coupled'` removed 2026-08-19).** `dγ/dt` is not an equation of motion — it is the rank-2 readout of the K-dimensional Glauber population dynamics (one `n_k` per visible target; see the project preprint's Glauber section). Differentiating `γ = Σₖ nₖ e^{iθ̂ₖ}` and substituting the population equation keeps only the `dnₖ/dt` term and drops

    −i·θ̇·Σₖ nₖ·U′(θₖ)·e^{iθ̂ₖ}

which is nonzero whenever the observer is turning (the neural angles move with the heading). Sridhar et al. 2021 SI Eq [15] drops the analogous term and is used only at fixed target bearings ("targets at infinity", SI §1.7); the preprint's own route is the timescale separation (its navigational model is driven by the equilibrium consensus γ*). **At θ̇ = 0 the term vanishes identically, so every SC equilibrium, `'reduced'` and `'discrim_a'` are all unaffected.** But taking the *full* 3×3 eigenvalues linearizes the incomplete equation. Measured on the vonmises k=0.55 island: the γ-3×3 flags 6 equilibria Hopf-unstable (with an attracting limit cycle of |γ|-amplitude 2.7e-2) where the exact `(n₁,n₂,θ)` system is stable at every one — under the current law and under both old torque laws (7 vs 0). It cannot be patched at the γ level: under a warp `U′` differs per target, so the dropped term is not proportional to γ. A genuine coupled analysis needs the `(n₁…n_K, θ)` system plus an explicit neural timescale `τ₀` — the verdict depends on `κτ₀`, and the γ-ODE implicitly assumes `κτ₀ = 2` (turning *faster* than neural relaxation), the opposite of the separation assumed everywhere else. `'reduced'` is the `τ₀ → 0` limit, which is what `plot_walkers` and `_basin_destination` integrate.

**Why `reduced` is the right default:** the simulated walker IS the slaved system (`plot_walkers` runs γ to equilibrium each step, then steps θ — `decision_model.py`:`dtheta=self.dtheta_dt()`), so its deterministic skeleton is the 1-D slow flow `dθ/dt = g(h(θ))` and its linear stability is exactly `discrim_a` (branch attracting) + Schur complement. Judging stability by the same reduction used to derive and integrate the model is the consistency argument; the full 3×3 answers a question about a *different* dynamical system the model never integrates. `discrim_a` (the fast block alone) is NOT equivalent to `reduced` — it omits the slow mode and so over-counts by exactly the fast-stable/slow-unstable equilibria. Validated in [tests/test_reduced_criterion.py](../../tests/test_reduced_criterion.py): block-determinant identity `det(J)=det(A)·λ_slow`, and `λ_slow` equals the independently-slaved slow-flow derivative `d/dθ[g(h(θ))]`.

**Self-consistent equilibria are exactly equilibria of the 3-eq system.** Proof: γ = R+0j gives `arg(γ) = 0`, so `dθ/dt = K·R·sin(0/2) = 0`. Combined with `dγ/dt = 0` from the search, all three RHS components vanish. So a `reduced`-unstable SC equilibrium is a real 3-eq equilibrium with a positive Jacobian eigenvalue, not a numerical artifact.

**`discrim_a` over-counts (worked example):** at (1.5, 0) in the vonmises k=0.55 setup, `reduced` reports 3 stable while `discrim_a` reports 5. The 2 extra `discrim_a`-"stable" equilibria at `θ ≈ ±0.16` are **fast-stable but slow-unstable**: the γ block `A` is Hurwitz, yet the slow Schur complement `λ_slow > 0` (≈ +0.26 under the current half-angle law; the matching positive eigenvalue in the full 3×3 is ≈ +0.18 — the finite-ε rescaling of the same root; was ≈ +0.31 under the old `sin(ego)` law). This is exactly the slow heading-tracking instability `discrim_a` cannot see. At (1.5, 0) `reduced` and `coupled` agree (no Hopf there); they diverge only inside Hopf regions, where `coupled` flags an oscillatory instability and `reduced` does not.

## Half-angle heading torque and the `K=2` default

The heading torque is the **half-angle** law `dθ/dt = K·R·sin(arg(γ)/2)` (NBM — the neural consensus angle) / `K·|γ|·sin(ego/2)` with `ego = convert_angles(angle(γ)−θ)` (IEM — physical angle difference). Rationale: a walker that has committed to a neural direction should turn *faster* the farther that direction is from its heading. `sin(ego)` was zero both straight-ahead (ego=0, the wanted SC equilibrium) and directly-behind (ego=±π) — the behind-zero was a spurious "dead zone" that let overshooting walkers wander off. `sin(ego/2)` is zero only at ego=0 and maximal at the facing-away point, so a walker facing away turns hard back toward consensus. There is an intentional **jump discontinuity** at ego=±π (torque flips `+K·R ↔ −K·R`), a real left/right fork resolved by roundoff/noise; the basin machinery treats it as a (branch-cut) basin-boundary event (see [theory/basins_of_attraction.md](../../theory/basins_of_attraction.md) §4). For NBM the SC turning equilibria are where `dθ/dt = K·R·sin(Θ/2) = 0` with `Θ = arg(γ)`: the smooth zero is `Θ = 0` (heading = consensus, stable); `Θ = ±π` (facing directly *away*) is that branch-cut fork, not a smooth equilibrium.

**Update (2026-06-02) — NBM torque moved to the neural angle.** NBM now uses the neural consensus angle `arg(γ)` directly (`sin(arg(γ)/2)`), dropping the inverse-warp mapping `ego = get_neural_angle_inverse(arg(γ))` that the old `sin(ego/2)` form applied. *Why:* in neural coordinates the facing-away heading is always `±π`, so the `÷2` normalization (zero ahead, maximal `±K·R` facing away) is **perception-model-independent** — the old ego form's effective normalization was instead the warp-dependent `ν(0)=W'(0)` (the neural density at center; ≈ "÷b" for a cutoff of half-width `b`). It also makes dθ/dt a direct function of γ with no ill-conditioned inverse near `±π`. *Invariant under the change:* SC-equilibrium locations, stability classification, stable/unstable counts, saddle-node curves, and the branch-cut jump magnitude `2·K·R` — the change rescales only the θ-row of the coupled Jacobian by the positive constant `ν(0)`, preserving eigenvalue signs. *Changed (θ-side magnitudes):* slow eigenvalues, `V''(θ_s)`, θ-relaxation timescales, θ-noise barriers `ΔV`, and Hopf-curve fine structure all rescale by `ν(0)` under a non-identity warp (e.g. vonmises k=0.55: ×1.609, confirmed). **dθ/dt is phenomenological and actively evolving:** treat θ-side calibration numbers in [theory/basins_of_attraction.md](../../theory/basins_of_attraction.md) and [VM_bifurcation_old_dtheta/VERDICT.md](../../VM_bifurcation_old_dtheta/VERDICT.md) as needing a re-run to be current; γ-side outputs (F̂, ΔF_γ, γ-folds, basin counts/widths) are turning-law-invariant and stand. IEM is unchanged (its `ego` is a physical angle difference, not warped).

**Default `K` was raised 1 → 2.** `sin(x/2)` has slope ½ at x=0 vs 1 for `sin(x)`, so K=2 restores the near-front turning gain. This is exact, not cosmetic: **at every SC equilibrium (ego=0) the K-doubling cancels the ½ in the full 3×3 coupled Jacobian** (the surviving partials are `K·R·cos(0)·½·∂ego/∂·`; the `sin(0)=0` terms drop), so eigenvalues — and therefore stability classification, Hopf and saddle-node locations, and every `plot_bifurcation_diagram` stable-count — are **identical to the old `K=1, sin(ego)` model**. Verified two ways in [tests/test_half_angle_torque.py](../../tests/test_half_angle_torque.py): a per-eq Jacobian-eigenvalue equality test, and an end-to-end stable-count raster that is bit-identical before/after. Only the *global* flow (walker trajectories, basin shapes off ego=0) changes. The IEM `convert_angles` wrap is load-bearing: `sin(x/2)` is 4π-periodic, so omitting it would make the torque wrong across 2π boundaries (regression-tested). The `K=2` invariance note concerns the earlier `sin(ego)`→`sin(ego/2)` half-angle change and is independent of the 2026-06-02 neural-angle move above.
