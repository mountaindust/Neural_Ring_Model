\maketitle

Reference note for the \texttt{'reduced'} stability criterion in
\texttt{decision\_model.py} (\texttt{NBM.\_discrim\_reduced} /
\texttt{IEM.\_discrim\_reduced}). Explains why the slow stability test is
evaluated as $\sign(\det J)$ rather than by inverting $A$.

\section{The identity}

For a block matrix with $A$ square and invertible and $d$ a scalar,
\[
J=\begin{pmatrix} A & b \\ c & d\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(J) = \det(A)\,\underbrace{\left(d - c\,A^{-1}b\right)}_{\lambda_{\text{slow}}}.
\]

The quantity $d - c A^{-1} b$ is the \textbf{Schur complement} of $A$ in $J$
(written $J/A$). In our $3\times 3$ coupled Jacobian on
$(\gamma_{\mathrm{re}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{im}}, \theta)$:

\begin{itemize}
  \item $A$ = the $2\times 2$ $\gamma$ block $\partial(d\gamma/dt)/\partial\gamma$,
  \item $b = \partial(d\gamma/dt)/\partial\theta$ ($2\times 1$ column),
  \item $c = \partial(d\theta/dt)/\partial\gamma$ ($1\times 2$ row),
  \item $d = \partial(d\theta/dt)/\partial\theta$ (scalar; $=0$ for NBM,
        $=-K\,R/2$ for IEM at an SC eq).
\end{itemize}

So $\lambda_{\text{slow}} = d - c A^{-1} b$ is a single real number.

\section{Proof (block LU factorization)}

Factor the coupling out of $J$:
\[
\begin{pmatrix} A & b \\ c & d\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix} I & 0 \\ c A^{-1} & 1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix} A & b \\ 0 & d - cA^{-1}b\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Check by multiplying out the bottom row of the product:
\begin{itemize}
  \item bottom-left: $cA^{-1}\cdot A + 1\cdot 0 = c$ \checkmark
  \item bottom-right: $cA^{-1}\cdot b + 1\cdot(d - cA^{-1}b) = d$ \checkmark
\end{itemize}

(top row is trivially $A$, $b$). The determinant of a product is the product
of determinants:
\begin{itemize}
  \item left factor: lower-triangular with unit diagonal blocks
        $\implies \det = 1\cdot 1 = 1$,
  \item right factor: upper block-triangular
        $\implies \det = \det(A)\cdot(d - cA^{-1}b)$.
\end{itemize}

Therefore $\det(J) = \det(A)\cdot(d - cA^{-1}b) = \det(A)\cdot\lambda_{\text{slow}}$.
\hfill$\blacksquare$

\section{Why $\lambda_{\text{slow}}$ is the slow eigenvalue}

In the timescale-separated (slaved) reduction, $\gamma$ is fast and tracks its
equilibrium branch $\gamma = h(\theta)$, leaving the 1-D slow flow
$d\theta/dt = g(h(\theta))$. Its linearization is the total derivative
\[
\lambda_{\text{slow}}
= \frac{d}{d\theta}\,g(h(\theta))
= \underbrace{d}_{\partial_\theta(d\theta/dt)}
+ \; c\,\underbrace{h'(\theta)}_{=\,-A^{-1}b}
= d - cA^{-1}b.
\]

$h'(\theta) = -A^{-1}b$ comes from differentiating the branch condition
$F(h(\theta), \theta) = 0$ in $\theta$. Reading it physically: perturb
$\theta \to$ the $\gamma$-equilibrium shifts by $h'(\theta) \to$ that shift
feeds back into $d\theta/dt$ through $c$. The direct term $d$ plus this
indirect $\gamma$-mediated term is the net slow restoring rate.

$\lambda_{\text{slow}}$ is also the leading ($\lambda \to 0$) term of the
\textbf{exact} eigenvalue equation $\lambda = d - c (A - \lambda I)^{-1} b$:
setting $\lambda = 0$ inside the resolvent is precisely the adiabatic
elimination of $\gamma$.

\section{How the criterion uses it}

$A$ is symmetric (the $\gamma$-dynamics is a gradient flow,
$A = -\Hess \hat F$), so its eigenvalues are real. Once the \textbf{Hurwitz
gate} passes (both $\eig(A) < -\mathrm{tol}$, i.e. the $\gamma$-branch is
attracting), the two eigenvalues are negative reals and
\[
\det(A) = \mu_1 \mu_2 > 0.
\]

With a positive prefactor, $\det(J)$ and $\lambda_{\text{slow}}$ share a sign:
\[
\boxed{\;\lambda_{\text{slow}} < 0 \iff \det(J) < 0\;}
\]

So the slow-mode stability test is just $\sign(\det J)$ --- \textbf{no
$A^{-1}$}. The criterion is therefore:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $A$ Hurwitz (fast/$\gamma$ layer attracting), \textbf{and}
  \item $\det(J) < 0$ (slow/$\theta$ layer stable).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why $\det(J)$ instead of computing $\lambda_{\text{slow}}$ directly}

Near a \textbf{$\gamma$-fold} (saddle-node of the fixed-heading
$\gamma$-equilibrium), one eigenvalue of $A \to 0$, so $A^{-1}$ --- and hence
$\lambda_{\text{slow}} = d - cA^{-1}b$ itself --- \textbf{diverges}
($|\lambda_{\text{slow}}| \to \infty$). The sign is still unambiguous, but the
literal Schur expression is numerically ill-conditioned.
$\det(J) = \det(A)\cdot\lambda_{\text{slow}}$ stays \textbf{bounded}
($\det(A) \to 0$ cancels the blow-up) and keeps the correct sign.

Measured at the island fold tip $\approx(2.467, 2.633)$:
$\min|\eig(A)| \approx 8\mathrm{e}{-4}$, $|\lambda_{\text{slow}}| \approx 308$,
while $\det(J) \approx -0.25$. Verdict-identical to the Schur form across the
island grid (0/538 mismatches); unit-tested in
\texttt{tests/test\_reduced\_criterion.py}
(\texttt{test\_schur\_block\_determinant\_identity\_*},
\texttt{test\_reduced\_robust\_near\_gamma\_fold}).

A useful structural note: the ill-conditioned and the delicate-decision
regimes are \textbf{anti-correlated}. Near a fold $|\lambda_{\text{slow}}|$ is
large (the slow mode is far from marginal, sign robust); a \emph{marginal}
slow eigenvalue ($\lambda_{\text{slow}} \approx 0$, where sign is delicate)
only occurs where $A$ is well-conditioned. You never face both difficulties at
once.
