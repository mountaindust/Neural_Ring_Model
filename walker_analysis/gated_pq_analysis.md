# Decoupling the walker noise exponents: `R^p` drift vs `(1−R)^q` gate

What the two exponents in the gated walker noise law actually do — separately —
for the three-target center-skew problem and for the target-circling problem,
backed by a `(p, q)` sweep. This is the empirical follow-up to
[three_target_analysis.md](three_target_analysis.md), which established the
center-target bias and the constant-noise levers; read that first.

The heading SDE (`NeuralBandModel._simulate_one_walk`) on a **visible** step is

```
theta  <-  theta  +  K·R^p · sin(Θ/2) · dt   +   σ · (1−R)^q · cos(Θ/2) · √dt · Z
                     \___ drift ___/             \________ noise _______/
```

with `R = |γ|`, `Θ` the consensus angle, `p = R_exp` (drift exponent on
coherence), `q = noise_exp` (gate exponent on `1−R`), `Z ~ N(0,1)`. The shipped
**default couples them**: `R_exp = 1` when `noise_exp = 0`, else `R_exp = 1/q`.
This study **decouples** `p` and `q` and sweeps both.

## The question

Two pathologies were in tension under the *coupled* defaults:

- **Constant noise (`q = 0`)** de-skews the three-target split (more noise →
  fewer center captures) but, at the σ needed, walkers **circle the target** —
  the noise never quiets on commitment, so the final approach wanders.
- **Default-gated noise (`q > 0`, `p = 1/q`)** quiets the noise as `R → 1`
  (clean homing, no circling) but is **center-heavy**: it shuts the escape noise
  off at the second bifurcation (`R ≈ 0.5`), so walkers get recaptured to the
  center target.

The hypothesis: these are two *different* knobs once `p` and `q` are decoupled.
The effective signal-to-noise of the heading dynamics is

```
SNR(R)  ∝  (K/σ) · R^p / (1−R)^q
```

- **High-R (homing):** `(1−R)^q → 0` makes SNR → ∞ → quiet target.
  Governed by **q** (independent of p) → the **circling** knob.
- **Mid-R (second bifurcation, R ≈ 0.5):** `SNR(0.5) ∝ (K/σ)·2^(q−p)`.
  Governed by **p−q** → the **center-skew** knob.

So `q ≥ 1` should kill circling, and raising `p` toward `q` should undo the
gate's center bias — letting a steep gate (clean tracks) coexist with a fair
split. (An earlier verbal analysis concluded "never combine `R^p`, `p>1` with a
`(1−R)^q` gate" because both go weak at mid-R and stall. The sweep shows that was
too strong — see "The predicted stall did not bite" below.)

## Setup

Data-fixed three-target locust reconstruction (as in
[three_target_analysis.md](three_target_analysis.md)): three `circle` targets
(`r = 0.3`) at 0°/+40°/−40°, radius 5; walker starts at the origin facing 0°
(dead at the center target); `neural_angle_dist='lin_cutoff'`
(`a_warp=0.25π, b_warp=0.9π`), `angle_weight='lin_cutoff'`
(`a_weight=0.25π, b_weight=0.30π`); `K=4.5, β=15, v=0.5, dt=0.1`. **60 seeds per
cell.** Endpoint classified to nearest target within 0.5; "none" = `max_steps`
(no capture). Circling is quantified by **tortuosity** = path length ÷ net
displacement (1.0 = straight line). Walkers are generated by calling
`NeuralBandModel._simulate_one_walk` directly (the production law, no
reimplementation), so results match `plot_walkers` seed-for-seed.

The center-skew target is **1:2:1 (top:center:bottom)** ⇔ **center fraction
0.50**. Tables below report center fraction (lower = less skew), capture rate,
and tortiousity.

## Result 1 — `q` is the circling knob, and it is a near-threshold switch

Hold `p = 1` and sweep σ. Constant noise (`q = 0`) de-skews only by wandering:
tortuosity blows up and capture collapses. Any `q ≥ 1` pins **both** flat at
*every* σ tested.

**Constant `q = 0` (p = 1):**

| σ | 0.8 | 1.2 | 1.6 | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| center frac | 0.45 | 0.42 | 0.47 | 0.30 | 0.00 | 0.00 |
| median tortuosity | 1.15 | 1.56 | 2.52 | 4.51 | 7.13 | — |
| capture rate | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0.98 | 0.78 | 0.03 | 0.00 |

De-skewing and circling are the **same axis** here: by the σ that pulls center
to 0.30 (σ=2.0), tortuosity is 4.5 and a fifth of walkers never land; by σ=2.5
the population has essentially failed.

**Gated `q = 2` (p = 0.5, the default `1/q`):**

| σ | 0.8 | 1.2 | 1.6 | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| center frac | 0.65 | 0.55 | 0.55 | 0.53 | 0.53 | 0.45 |
| median tortuosity | 1.05 | 1.05 | 1.05 | 1.05 | 1.04 | 1.04 |
| capture rate | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

The gate completely **decouples σ from circling**: tortuosity ~1.05 and capture
1.00 regardless of σ. The threshold is sharp — at σ=2.0, across the full grid:

| q | 0.0 | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| capture rate | 0.81 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| median tortuosity | 5.23 | 1.04 | 1.04 | 1.04 | 1.05 |

**Any `q ≥ 1` is clean; `q = 0` is the only bad value.** `p` barely touches
tortuosity (in the `q=0` column, raising p 0.5→3 nudges median tortuosity
1.57→1.73 — drift suppression lets the walker wander a hair more, but it is a
rounding error next to the q effect). **This is the user's circling fix,
confirmed and unconditional in q.**

## Result 2 — σ is the de-skew strength; the gate is what makes a de-skewing σ usable

Center fraction falls with σ in every column, but only the gated columns survive
the σ a fair split needs. At σ=2.0 the constant law is at center 0.30 *but
broken* (cap 0.81, tortuosity 4.5); the gated law is clean (cap 1.00, tortuosity
1.04) and already near-fair (0.42–0.53 depending on p, q). **Raise σ with the
gate on** is the core recipe — exactly the move the gate was invented to permit.

## Result 3 — `p` is a real, secondary de-skew knob, strongest where the gate is steep

Within the gated region, raising `p` reduces the center share, and the effect is
**largest at steep gates (high q)** — precisely where the default `p = 1/q`
leaves the decision-zone SNR most center-biased. Converged center fractions
(σ=2.0, **dt=0.025**):

| p \ q | 1.5 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| **0.5** (≈ default `1/q`) | 0.43 | **0.57** |
| **2.0** | 0.48 | 0.48 |
| **3.0** | 0.45 | **0.48** |

At the steep gate `q = 2`, the default `p = 0.5` is center-heavy (0.57 ≈
1 : 2.4 : 1); decoupling `p` up to 2–3 pulls it to 0.48 ≈ **1 : 2 : 1** — a
dt-robust ~0.09 correction, in the predicted direction (`SNR(0.5) ∝ 2^(q−p)`).
At the gentler gate `q = 1.5` the default is already near-fair and the p-effect
is small. (Sign caveat: `q` *also* pushes center up on its own beyond the
mid-point proxy — `q=1` runs slightly outer-heavy, `q=2` slightly center-heavy —
so `p−q` predicts the *sign* of both knobs but does not quantitatively collapse
the grid; the gate shape matters over the whole `R∈[0.5,1]` commitment leg, not
just at R=0.5.)

**Magnitude / significance.** The p-effect is modest: ~0.05–0.10 in center
fraction, ≈1 binomial SE at 60 seeds per cell, so any single cell is weak. What
makes it credible is **consistency of sign** across σ, across q≥2, and across
three dt values (raising p never raised center on average). Treat p as a
**fine-tuning** knob, not a dramatic lever; the unambiguous, large effect is the
q/circling decoupling in Result 1.

## Result 4 — higher `p` also makes the ratio dt-robust

The default-gated cells (low `p = 1/q`, i.e. *boosted* drift `R^{1/q}`) are the
dt-sensitive ones; suppressing the drift with higher `p` stabilizes the ratio.
Center fraction across dt (σ=2.0, q=2):

| p | dt=0.1 | dt=0.05 | dt=0.025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 (default) | 0.53 | 0.62 | 0.57 |
| 2.0 | 0.52 | 0.55 | 0.48 |
| 3.0 | 0.50 | 0.48 | **0.48** |

and at q=1.5: p=0.5 swings 0.45→0.55→0.43 (range 0.12) while p=3 is flat
0.45→0.45→0.45. Mechanism: the drift step is `K·R^p·sin(Θ/2)·dt`, so the binding
`K·dt` per-step turn *shrinks* as p rises (R<1) → smaller Euler error. The
boosted-drift default (`R^{1/q}`) does the opposite, which is why
[three_target_analysis.md](three_target_analysis.md) flagged the gated law as
dt-sensitive. **Decoupling p upward both de-skews and converges.**

## The predicted stall did not bite

The verbal worry was that `R^p` (p>1) drift plus a steep `(1−R)^q` gate makes
*both* channels weak at mid-R → the walker stalls, inflating "none" and path
length. **It did not happen.** Across the entire gated grid (`q ≥ 1`, all p up to
3), capture stayed **1.00**, median tortuosity **1.04–1.05**, and steps-to-capture
flat (~97–98 at dt=0.1). The only "none" inflation and path blow-up anywhere is
the `q = 0` constant column at high σ — i.e. circling, not stalling. For this
geometry the walker's coherence rises fast enough through the decision zone
(extended `r = 0.3` targets saturate R near commitment) that the mid-R window is
a brief transit, not a trap. So the combination is safe here; the stall remains a
*potential* failure mode for harder geometries (zero-radius/delta targets, or p
pushed well past 3) and is worth a capture-rate check if those are used.

## Practical recipe for a clean 1:2:1

All require **`q ≥ 1`** (clean tracks) — that is non-negotiable; `q = 0` circles
at any de-skewing σ.

1. **Pick `q ≈ 1.5–2`** to kill circling (capture 1.00, tortuosity ~1.05).
2. **Raise σ to ~2.0** to de-skew; the gate keeps it clean (constant noise is
   already failing at this σ — cap 0.81).
3. **If using a steep gate (`q = 2`), set `p ≈ 2–3`** (decoupled from the default
   `1/q = 0.5`) to cancel its center bias and gain dt-robustness. With a gentler
   gate (`q = 1.5`) the default p is already near-fair and p matters less.

Converged, dt-robust cells that land ≈ **1 : 2 : 1** (σ=2.0, dt=0.025, clean,
capture 1.00):

| cell | census (c:u:l) | center frac |
|---|---|---|
| `q=2, p=2` | 29 : 15 : 16 | 0.48 |
| `q=2, p=3` | 29 : 16 : 15 | 0.48 |
| `q=1.5, p=2` | 29 : 15 : 16 | 0.48 |
| `q=1.5, p=3` | 27 : 16 : 17 | 0.45 |

versus the default-gated control `q=2, p=0.5`: 34 : 11 : 15 (center 0.57 ≈
1 : 2.4 : 1, and dt-sensitive). Up/down differences of a few counts are seed
sampling, not real asymmetry.

## Summary table — what each knob does

| knob | primary effect | direction | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **q** (`(1−R)^q` gate) | **circling / capture** | `q ≥ 1` → clean (tort ~1.05, cap 1.00); `q = 0` → circles & fails at de-skewing σ | near-threshold switch; also pushes center *up* (steeper gate cuts escape noise) |
| **σ** (`std`) | **de-skew strength** | ↑σ → less center | usable only with `q ≥ 1`; constant noise circles/fails at the needed σ |
| **p** (`R^p` drift) | **center fine-tune + dt-robustness** | ↑p → modestly less center; ↑p → dt-converged | strongest at steep gates (`q ≥ 2`); cancels the default `p=1/q` center bias; magnitude ~0.05–0.10 (use as fine-tune) |
| `p − q` | sign of the decision-zone SNR `∝ 2^(q−p)` | `p ≳ q` → fair; `p ≪ q` (the default) → center-heavy | qualitative; does not collapse the grid quantitatively |

**Bottom line.** Decoupling the exponents resolves the two-problems-at-once bind:
the `(1−R)^q` gate (`q ≥ 1`) is an unconditional circling fix that lets you raise
σ to de-skew, and raising the drift exponent `p` to ≈ `q` removes the steep-gate
center bias *and* makes the ratio dt-robust. The earlier "never combine `p>1`
with a gate" caution was wrong for this regime — the stall it feared never
materializes here (capture 1.00, tortuosity 1.05 across the whole gated grid).
The constant-noise law cannot reach any clean fair point; the decoupled
`R^p`/`(1−R)^q` law (`q≈1.5–2, p≈2–3, σ≈2.0`) can.

## Method

Each cell was sampled by calling `NeuralBandModel._simulate_one_walk` over the
grid `p = R_exp ∈ {0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3}` × `q = noise_exp ∈ {0, 1, 1.5, 2,
2.5}`, 60 seeds/cell, with the Setup parameters above; each endpoint classified
to the nearest target within 0.5 (none = `max_steps` exceeded) and tortuosity =
path length ÷ net displacement. Two σ regimes (1.2 and 2.0) mapped the landscape
at dt=0.1; the clean high-σ cells in Results 3–4 were re-run at dt=0.05 and 0.025
(with `max_steps` scaled to hold total integration time fixed) as the
convergence check, and Result 1's σ-window scan swept std ∈ {0.8…3.0} at
reference cells. The one-off sweep harness and its raw `.npz` rows were not
retained — the tables above are the durable record.
